# ROB7103/8103 — assessment guide

**Autumn 2026 · Published 20 October 2026**

Your grade combines individual attendance (10%) and one team project (90%). The project is developed through six checkpoints and communicated through code/results, final worksheets, video, poster and live presentation. Team components receive a shared team mark; attendance is individual. The criteria below explain the allocation within each component; the published category weights are unchanged.

## What is assessed

Points below are percentage points of the final course grade. Each component is marked separately against the evidence submitted for that component.

| Component | Course points | Criteria and point allocation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Individual attendance | 10 | Recorded attendance at eligible scheduled meetings, calculated proportionally. Public-holiday non-meetings do not count. Instructor-approved absence arrangements apply. |
| Six worksheet checkpoints, Classes 12–17 | 30 | Each worksheet: focus **2**, evidence/traceability **1**, reasoning/next step **1**, clear and complete team review **1**. **5 points per worksheet.** |
| Working system and reproducibility package | 25 | Implementation/integration **8**; experiments/evidence **7**; reproduction package **7**; attribution/limitations **3**. |
| Six final updated worksheets | 10 | Consistency across the six worksheets **4**; revision/reflection **3**; evidence traceability **2**; complete final review **1**. |
| Video | 10 | Question/system explanation **3**; visible and interpretable evidence **4**; conclusion/limitations **2**; communication/specification **1**. |
| Live presentation and Q&A | 10 | Coherent live explanation **3**; technical understanding **4**; answers/scientific judgment **3**. |
| Poster | 5 | Scientific message **2**; visual explanation **2**; identity/attribution/specification **1**. |
| **Total** | **100** | **Individual 10 + team 90.** |

## What each checkpoint should establish

| Worksheet | Main focus for its 2-point criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Class 12 — Claim | A bounded question, intended capability, comparison and feasible success criterion. |
| Class 13 — Experience | The interaction episode, observations, actions, reset, outcome and collection coverage. |
| Class 14 — System | Physical components and interfaces, including units, frames, timing and operating boundaries. |
| Class 15 — Grounding | Measurement meaning, calibration/correspondence checks, uncertainty and validation conditions. |
| Class 16 — Learning | The chosen learning/inference approach, data use, baseline, evaluation and observed limitations. |
| Class 17 — Decision | A warranted conclusion, critical unknowns and a justified next action. |

For every checkpoint, distinguish completed work from plans and cite relevant repository paths. Explain unfinished items and the next feasible check. Early checkpoints can earn full marks for strong, feasible design reasoning supported by the evidence appropriate to that stage; they do not require a completed final system. Later claims about implementation, calibration or learning need corresponding executed evidence. Merely promising an experiment does not establish its outcome.

Complete the required pages, answer applicable prompts or explain exclusions, identify your team and obtain every member's signature. Worksheet evidence-support ratings and E1–E5 context labels are reasoning aids: they are **not course marks and are not averaged into a grade**.

## How performance is marked

Each criterion uses the same five levels. Awarded points = criterion maximum × level ÷ 4. Markers select the level using the criterion's stated expectation, evidence and project stage. Component scores are summed; the final course total is rounded once to two decimal places.

| Level | Description |
| ---: | --- |
| **4 — Strong** | Clear, technically sound and well justified; evidence is traceable and appropriate to the claim/stage; consequential limits are handled. |
| **3 — Satisfactory** | The criterion is substantially met; remaining gaps are minor and do not undermine the central explanation or result. |
| **2 — Developing** | A meaningful contribution is present, but important gaps limit interpretation, execution or checking. |
| **1 — Limited** | Relevant work is present, but major omissions or unsupported reasoning make it difficult to assess. |
| **0 — Not demonstrated** | No assessable work addresses this criterion, or the submitted material provides no support for meeting it. |

The attendance calculation is separate: **10 × attended eligible meetings ÷ eligible scheduled meetings**, subject to instructor-approved absence arrangements. There is no attendance requirement for the Class 19/20 public-holiday non-meetings.

## What good final evidence looks like

- **System/code:** an implemented contribution within your agreed physical-project scope, a defined experiment/comparison, interpretable records and a README identifying the tested environment, required assets, commands, configuration and expected outputs. Another reader should be able to reproduce a central result or audit preserved evidence when hardware is unavailable. An audit alone does not demonstrate successful reproduction or operation.
- **Final worksheets:** describe the same final project and explain how evidence changed or confirmed earlier choices. No change is required merely to appear revised. These marks assess integration and reflection; checkpoint marks remain tied to the earlier collected work.
- **Video:** explain the project without a live presenter; show relevant results and limits. Label simulation, replay, human assistance, edits and speed changes when present. Use the existing MP4/1080p/four-minute requirements.
- **Presentation/Q&A:** explain the project live and answer with evidence. Relevant members may answer; every member should understand the overall project and their contribution. No extra slide deck or file is required.
- **Poster:** communicate the question, system, evidence and bounded conclusion through readable text and meaningful figures. Use the existing A0 portrait PDF requirements.

Technical success alone does not earn full marks. A failed or negative experiment can earn strong marks for sound design, reliable records, diagnosis and justified conclusions. It cannot establish a capability that was not demonstrated: the implementation and evidence criteria still apply. Expensive hardware, large datasets, new network architectures, publication-level novelty and successful live hardware operation at the showcase are not independent marking criteria. Follow the project scope and demonstration plan agreed with the instructor.

## Submission and fair handling

Use the [course deadlines](../student.md#important-deadlines), [worksheet submission guide](project-workbook/README.md) and [final-artifact instructions](../student.md#start-here) for exact filenames, folders and deadlines. Submit in your assigned private team repository. Push to remote `main`; the teaching team assesses the collected revision using GitHub push timestamps. Late work, access problems and approved exceptions are referred to the instructor under the applicable course/University arrangements; this guide introduces no new late-penalty scale.

File, page, signature and format issues are recorded and assessed only where relevant to the stated criterion. There is no separate blanket administrative deduction or duplicate deduction for the same issue. If a file is unreadable or evidence cannot be accessed, the teaching team records what could and could not be assessed. Readable work is still assessed on its merits.

Cite reused code, data, models, media and research; distinguish your contribution and label evidence honestly. Suspected academic-integrity issues follow the University's process rather than an improvised rubric penalty. Awards do not alter grades. No additional student forms or submissions are introduced by this guide.
