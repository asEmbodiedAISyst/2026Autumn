# 01 Course Introduction with asMagicBrain

#### Purpose

Introduce the course's physical–digital experimentation pathway, eight-team organization, source repositories, evidence standards, and final project.

#### Learning objectives

* Distinguish a motor from an integrated actuator, its sensing, drive electronics, transmission, and controller.
* Distinguish a command, an internal reference, and a measured response.
* Read model-specific actuator specifications and operating limits.

#### Lecture — 40 minutes

1. Course questions; embodiment and controlled action; the six-stage project record; demonstration versus student simulation; expectations for reproducibility and attribution.

#### Break — 5 minutes

Use your two-digit team number in place of `NN`, for example `Team01-poster.pdf` and `Team01-video.mp4`. The teaching team appends `-YYMMDD-HHMMSS` to collected filenames, using the GitHub-recorded submission push time in Abu Dhabi time (UTC+4), for example `Team01-poster-261127-104530.pdf`. Students keep code in the existing repository; instructors create `Team01-code-YYMMDD-HHMMSS.zip`.

Commit and push the deliverable to `main` before its deadline, then inspect the files on GitHub. A saved local file or unpushed commit is not available for collection. At each deadline the teaching team captures the remote revision, records its commit, GitHub push time and collection time separately, and retains that revision. The deadline concerns when the version reached GitHub, not its local commit date. Later changes remain later work; they do not replace the collected snapshot.

No release, tag, manifest or separate student collection record is required. The folders are the submission route. Classes 18–20 explain preparation of each artifact; these are progressive opportunities to finalize the same project.

#### Instructor/TA demonstration — 25 minutes

Show the published course and one assigned team repository. Open its README and the three submission folders, distinguish local saving, committing and pushing, and inspect a pushed revision on GitHub. Explain which repository state the teaching team collects at a deadline. Show a prepared physical platform, MuJoCo and a sample dataset as examples of the course pathway. asMagicBrain may be shown as the instructor’s authoring tool; students can use GitHub/GitBook directly.

#### Student activity — 10 minutes

Form eight teams. Each team identifies its assigned repository, confirms authorized access, locates the poster/code/video folders and identifies the three deadlines. Raise an access problem with the teaching team before project work depends on that repository.
