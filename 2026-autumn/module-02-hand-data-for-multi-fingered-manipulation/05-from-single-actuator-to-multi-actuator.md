# 05 From Single-Actuator to Multi-Actuator

#### Central question

Why does matching one actuator not establish the behavior of a multi-actuator system?

#### Lecture — 40 minutes

1. Review the single-XC330 analysis and declared validation task.
2. Distinguish simultaneously operated independent fixtures from mechanically coupled joints in a robot.
3. Shared communication, supply, timing, load and thermal effects; later add contact and mechanical coupling.
4. Explain the neural-actuator approach and its original research context before discussing its possible use here.
5. Introduce the supplied 16-XC330 campaign, controller conditions, split roles and per-motor comparisons.
6. Internal encoders versus external optical measurements and their calibration requirements.

#### Break — 5 minutes

#### Instructor/TA demonstration — 25 minutes

Show the single-actuator fixture and matched before/after evidence, then the 16-motor collection arrangement and prepared traces. Show original asMagic camera/marker records. The current package identifies encoder position as the measured output; camera videos are supporting evidence until calibrated to joint angle.

#### Student activity — 10 minutes

Compare two motors or operating conditions using prepared data. Decide which differences support individual models and which remain unexplained.

#### Existing material and limits

The supplied package reports 125 simultaneous science episodes, 32 motor/controller fits and held-out comparisons. It still needs a teaching release and numerical verification. Reported bound-reaching parameters are effective closed-loop fits, not isolated motor constants. The large Model2Prototype source/media archive is not part of routine student installation.

#### Follow-up

Continue analysis during project time. Improvement of LEAP behavior requires a separate in-hand experiment; fixture fits alone do not establish transfer.
