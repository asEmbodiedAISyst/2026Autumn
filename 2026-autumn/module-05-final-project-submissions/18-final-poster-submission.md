# 18 Final Poster Submission

## How we will use this class

**Most of this class is reserved for final-project and poster preparation with your team.** After a short introduction, work on the connection between your claim, system, evidence and conclusion. Use the examples below when deciding what the poster should show, and discuss unclear claims or figures with the teaching team. Use the working time to improve the project evidence and finish the poster together.

**Before class ends, each team must submit its final poster PDF.** Push `poster/TeamNN-poster.pdf` to your assigned private team repository's `main` branch by the deadline below. Reserve time to open the uploaded PDF, check its layout and confirm that it is the version your team intends to submit for printing. The poster is the required submission for this class; follow the requirements below.

**Deadline: 27 November 2026, 10:50 — Abu Dhabi time (UTC+4).**

## Submission requirements

The [RSS 2026 poster instructions](https://roboticsconference.org/2026/information/presentationInstructions/) specify:

> A0 (841 wide x 1189mm high) and portrait orientation.

For this course, submit one **PDF**. Name it `TeamNN-poster.pdf`, replacing `NN` with your two-digit team number: `01`–`08`. Team 01 submits `poster/Team01-poster.pdf`. Include your team identifier (**Team01–Team08**), project title and authors on the poster. The teaching team prints posters submitted by the deadline; teams arrange printing for late submissions.

## How to submit and how we collect

Commit and push the PDF to the `main` branch of your assigned team repository before the deadline. Open the uploaded PDF on GitHub to confirm it is available.

At the deadline, the teaching team records the remote `main` commit, collects the required files from that revision and retains the snapshot. We record the GitHub push time for the submitted version and the collection time separately, in Abu Dhabi time (UTC+4). The deadline concerns when the version reached GitHub, not its local commit date. Later pushes do not replace the collected version. Confirm teaching-team access before the deadline; report an access problem promptly.

For our collected copy, we use `TeamNN-poster-YYMMDD-HHMMSS.pdf`. **Keep your submitted filename as `TeamNN-poster.pdf`; do not add a timestamp.** The teaching team adds the suffix using the GitHub-recorded submission push time, not the collection time. No separate upload, email, release, tag, manifest or student collection record is required.

### Optional preparation references

- Amanda Butler, Amy Chan and Helen Lycett (2017), [Using design principles to inform scientific posters](https://blogs.nature.com/blog/using-design-principles-to-inform-scientific-posters/) — This Nature-hosted article gives practical advice on layout, concise text, visual balance and readability at viewing distance.
- Nicolas P. Rougier, Michael Droettboom and Philip E. Bourne (2014), [Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833) — Illustrated examples explain clear plots, captions, colour and avoiding misleading graphics for poster figures.
- Nature, [Nature research figure guide](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/) — This official guide covers accessible colours, resolution, panel export and image integrity when preparing finished artwork.

Optional tips: Thomas C. Erren and Philip E. Bourne, [“Ten Simple Rules for a Good Poster Presentation”](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030102), PLOS Computational Biology (2007).
