# 02 Actuator Basics for Controlled Action

#### Central question

What turns a motor into an actuator capable of controlled action?

#### Learning objectives

* Distinguish a motor from an integrated actuator, its sensing, drive electronics, transmission, and controller.
* Distinguish a command, an internal reference, and a measured response.
* Read model-specific actuator specifications and operating limits.

#### Lecture

1. Controlled action as a physical feedback process.
2. Position, velocity, and current/torque control; requested quantities are not automatically measured.
3. ROBOTIS XC330 and XL330: what “smart” means and what differs between specific models.
4. RoboStride RS06 and RS00: proprioceptive actuation and quasi-direct-drive concepts; other motor families only briefly.
5. Datasheets, user manuals and safety instructions: units, interfaces, limits, operating conditions and missing information.

#### Break

#### Instructor/TA demonstration

Trace application → asBrain/interface → actuator → telemetry. Identify a connected actuator, show bounded commands in supported modes, and inspect the responses. Compare RS06/RS00 documentation and the actual station configuration. The teaching team operates the powered actuator.

#### Student activity

Each student inspects one unpowered XC330, then returns it. Identify the model, output, connectors, and available feedback. Teams complete a short XC330/XL330 comparison using official documentation. The teaching team may show other actuator examples if available.

#### Optional follow-up

Locate three research examples using XC330/XL330. Record the exact actuator model and role; report when an exact-model source cannot be verified.
