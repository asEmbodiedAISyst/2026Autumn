# 12 Final Project I: Claim

## How we will use this class

**Most of this class is reserved for final-project preparation with your team.** After a short introduction, use the working time to refine your project claim, its scope, comparison and next feasible experiment. Discuss questions and evidence with the teaching team as you work. The readings and filled examples below are references for your project; use them to support your decisions and worksheet entries.

**Before class ends, each team must submit Worksheet 1: Claim.** Complete it by hand, obtain every member's signature, scan all required pages into one readable PDF, and push it to your assigned private team repository's `main` branch by the deadline below. Leave enough time to scan, upload and check the PDF on GitHub. This is a checkpoint of your current project work: where evidence or a result is missing, explain what is unfinished and your next step. You can revise the worksheet later while preserving this class's submitted version.

### Prepare and submit

1. Complete the worksheet by hand. Answer each prompt, or explain what is unfinished and what you will do next. Every member signs the team-review page.
2. Scan all required pages into **one readable PDF**. Check page order, orientation and that nothing is cropped. Keep the paper original for your records.
3. Name the PDF as shown above and put it in the specified folder in **your assigned private repository**. Reference supporting evidence by its existing repository path; do not copy it into another submission form.
4. Commit and **push to `main` before the deadline**. Open the PDF on GitHub and confirm every page is present and readable. A file saved only on your computer or an unpushed commit is not submitted.

### How the teaching team collects it

At the deadline, the teaching team records the remote `main` commit, collects the required files from that revision and retains the snapshot. We record the GitHub push time for the submitted version and the collection time separately, in Abu Dhabi time (UTC+4). The deadline concerns when the version reached GitHub, not its local commit date. Later pushes do not replace the collected version. Confirm teaching-team access before the deadline; report an access problem promptly.

**No separate upload, email, release, tag, manifest or physical hand-in is required.** Later changes do not replace the collected version. Keep the initial PDF; save later dated, signed revisions in `submissions/class12/revisions/`.

For the 10% consolidated and updated project record, place your final signed copy in `worksheets/` by **2 December 2026 at 11:50 (UTC+4)**, following the [Class 20 final worksheet instructions](../module-05-final-project-submissions/20-final-video-submission.md#final-updated-worksheets). Keep this class's collected checkpoint unchanged.

## Confirm your project scope

Confirm the proposed physical/simulation scope and final demonstration plan with the instructor, following the [course assessment guidance](../student.md#project-scope-and-assessment-guidance). A bounded simulation claim can be scientifically useful without automatically satisfying the final project's physical-prototype expectation. Record the agreed scope, remaining decisions and next feasible test on your Claim worksheet. Check the instructor's marking criteria as well as the submission checklist; evidence-support labels are not course marks.

## Reading and project guidance

Choose one observable capability and state the conditions, measure and comparison that would test it. Keep two candidate claims, select the narrower one and identify the next feasible experiment. A planned test is acceptable; distinguish it from a result.

Use the examples below to understand how to answer the prompts, then complete the blank worksheet for **your own project**. Yellow backgrounds identify the filled example entries. These are concise readings of published work, not independent reproductions or complete audits; distinguish a paper’s reported result from your team’s evidence.
